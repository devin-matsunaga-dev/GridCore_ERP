- **⚠️ AN ARRANGEMENT CREATES NO MONEY, MUTATES NO BILL AND PUBLISHES NO EVENT.** The customer still owes what the bills say; this records how and when it will arrive. WP-2.21 reads it through this module, so there is nothing downstream for an event to be true of — the rule WP-2.15, WP-2.16 and WP-2.19 all followed. A fast test asserts that proposing, activating and settling publish nothing at all.
- **⚠️ AN INSTALMENT IS SETTLED BY CONSUMING `PaymentApproved`, SO SETTLEMENT IS EVENTUALLY CONSISTENT.** Customers has published since WP-1.1 and never subscribed; this is the other direction. **Any** approved payment on the account counts, not only one taken against an arranged bill — an arrangement is about the arrears as a whole. Billing, Finance and now Customers all claim this event under **distinct consumer names**; a shared one would have whichever handled it first silently suppress the others. The gate suite proves the bill's balance falls *and* the instalment settles from one payment.
- **⚠️ THE REP'S LIMIT IS TWO CEILINGS, KEYED ON CUSTOMER CLASS, AND NOT EFFECTIVE-DATED.** $1,500 over 6 instalments residential, $5,000 over 12 commercial — **all demo figures, each saying so in its own row**, because CUC publishes that Customer Service will arrange payment and does not publish what a representative may sign alone. Raising one is a migration, not a redeploy.
- **⚠️ WP-0.4's APPROVAL PRIMITIVE HAS ITS FIRST MODULE CONSUMER.** An over-limit arrangement raises an `ApprovalRequest` of type `customers.payment_arrangement` naming **`customers.arrange`** as what a decider must hold — which with `platform.approve` is a Manager and not the rep who raised it. The request is raised **inside the proposal's unit of work**, so the arrangement and the request that decides it commit together. Activation reads the decision off the queue rather than mirroring it; a rejected approval leaves the arrangement in `Proposed` for ever, to be replaced rather than revived.
- **⚠️ THE STANDING IS COMPUTED AND THE REVIEW RUN WRITES IT DOWN.** `PaymentArrangement.StandingOn(asOf)` is what the disconnection seam reads, so an account defaulting on a Friday stops being protected immediately rather than waiting for a job. `POST /api/payment-arrangements/reviews` persists exactly what that function answers, so the stored status and the computed one can never disagree — and it audits **each arrangement it breaks against that arrangement**, because breaking one restores disconnection eligibility. A **stored** terminal status wins outright: an arrangement already recorded as broken stays broken however much arrives afterwards.
- **⚠️ THE SCHEDULE'S REMAINDER LANDS ON THE LAST INSTALMENT, AND THE DIVISION TRUNCATES.** $100 over three is 33.33 / 33.33 / **33.34**. Rounding half away from zero could make the equal instalments exceed the balance and force the last line negative — a credit in the middle of a promise to pay. The down payment is a **line of the schedule**, due the day it is made and counted in the sum; one covering the whole balance is refused as "take the payment instead". The aggregate re-asserts the total before it accepts a schedule.
- **⚠️ AN ARRANGEMENT MAY NOT PROMISE MORE THAN THE ACCOUNT'S PAST-DUE BALANCE → 409.** Past due, not outstanding: a bill issued last week and due next month is not something anybody is behind with. The arranged figure is **stamped**, never re-read. **One promise at a time** per account — but an arrangement that has quietly defaulted does not block a fresh one, or a customer who missed one instalment could not arrange again until a job ran.

- **Cross-context atomicity is settled (WP-0.5).** A module registers its context with `services.AddGridCoreDbContext<TContext>((builder, connection) => builder.UseNpgsql(connection, ...))` — never plain `AddDbContext`, which would give it its own connection and its own transaction. That is what makes a module write + its audit entry + its outbox row atomic.
