## In flight / carry-over
- **Bill adjustments are live (WP-2.4)** — `billing.bill_adjustments`, migration `WP24_BillAdjustments`, one endpoint `POST /api/bills/{id}/adjustments` gated on **`billing.adjust`**, which until now opened nothing. A fast test asserts it is the *only* route behind that permission, and that the policy differs from both `billing.read` and `billing.generate` — WP-0.3 gave Managers `billing.adjust` **without** `billing.generate`, so the caller who may credit a disputed bill is not the caller who may raise one.
- **⚠️ There is no `Adjusted` status, against `WORK_PACKAGES.md` and `SPEC.md`** (owner's call, taken before any code was written). An adjustment is a financial correction, not a lifecycle state: the bill stays `Issued`/`PartiallyPaid`/`Overdue` and the correction lives in immutable entries plus the audit trail. `BillStatus` and `BillTransitions` are **untouched**. **Both spec files still say "Adjusted" and were deliberately not edited** — reconciling them is the owner's call, and a good candidate for WP-2.7's tidy-up.
- **A credit that clears the balance still moves the bill to `Paid`.** Not the `Adjusted` state by another door: nothing is owed any more, and leaving it `Issued` parks a zero-balance row on the AR worklist for good. It goes through the same `Move` the machine guards. **`AmountPaid` is not touched** — settled by a credit is not paid, and WP-2.6's ledger must not be told money arrived.
- **⚠️ `Bill.TotalAmount` and what a customer owes are now two different figures.** `TotalAmount` is what the rate engine printed and never moves again; **`Bill.AmountDue` = `TotalAmount + AdjustmentTotal`**, and **`Balance` is now `AmountDue - AmountPaid`**, not `TotalAmount - AmountPaid`. Anything summing AR must use `Balance` or `AmountDue`. WP-2.5's payment consumer and WP-2.6's ledger both land on this.
- **⚠️ `AdjustmentTotal` is a stored column and `Bill.Adjust` refuses to run without the whole history in hand.** A list does not load a bill's adjustments, so the running total has to be a column — and a column that can disagree with its rows needs a guard, so `Adjust` throws unless the loaded entries sum to it. **`BillService.LoadAsync` includes `Adjustments`; any later path that loads a bill to write to it owes the same `Include`.** Same shape as `Bill.Calculate` refusing a total that disagrees with its own lines.
- **A kind and a positive amount, never a signed figure a caller types.** `Credit` and `Charge`; `BillAdjustment.Signed` applies the sign and refuses an unknown enum value rather than defaulting to a direction. The **stored** amount is signed, so summing the history reproduces what is owed. WP-1.4's stock-take call, applied to money.
- **A credit larger than the balance is refused (409)**, and `Draft`, `Paid` and `Cancelled` bills cannot be adjusted at all. Crediting past zero leaves a credit balance, which is Finance's to hold (WP-2.6) — the same call WP-2.3 made about an overpayment.
- **`BillAdjusted` (`Contracts/Events/`) is published through the outbox and nothing consumes it yet** — WP-2.6's Finance consumer picks it up, exactly as `BillIssued` waited from WP-0.5. It states a **signed change** to a known amount plus the resulting `AmountDue`, which is what lets a consumer post a second balanced entry rather than rewrite the first.
- **No approval workflow here** (owner's call): `billing.adjust` alone. `ApprovalQueueDemoSeeder`'s pending `billing.adjustment` request is still decorative — **WP-4.4 owns wiring the inbox to it.**
- **Audited against the bill, not the entry** — `AuditActions.BillAdjusted` (`bill.adjust`) with `AuditEntityTypes.Bill`. `BillSnapshot` gained `AdjustmentTotal` and `AmountDue`, so the before/after shows the money moving while `TotalAmount` visibly does not.
- **The demo world now opens with two corrected bills**, one credited and one charged, both through the real aggregate. A world with fewer than two outstanding bills on two accounts seeds none rather than throwing.
- **`/billing` is still a `ModulePlaceholder` and its `owners` label in `web/src/routes/routes.tsx` still reads `WP-2.3`** — stale since WP-2.3 shipped no screens. Left alone as out of scope; **whoever builds the billing screens should fix or claim it** (WP-2.7 names a demo screen).
- **Billing is live (WP-2.3)** — `billing.bills`, `billing.bill_lines`, `billing.account_rate_plans`, migrations `WP23_RatePlanVersions` and `WP23_Bills`, endpoints under `/api/bills`, `/api/rate-plans` and `/api/account-rate-plans`, all gated on the pre-existing `billing.read`/`billing.generate`. **`billing.adjust` is still claimed by nothing — WP-2.4 owns it**, and a fast test asserts no route demands it yet.
- **⚡ CONVENTIONS.md's central rounding helper finally exists: `Platform/Monetary/Money.cs`.** Four WPs deferred it here. Rounding is **half away from zero**, not `decimal.Round`'s banker's default — a customer checking by hand rounds 0.125 up, and the demonstrable rule beats the statistically neutral one wherever a person has to agree with the answer. **It does not license truncation:** WP-1.1's deposit, WP-1.3's coordinate, WP-1.4's quantity and WP-2.1's installation reading still *refuse* an over-precise value, and those guards were left alone deliberately. `Money.IsRounded` is how a guard asks. **Note the namespace is `Monetary`, not `Money`** — a type whose name matches its own namespace is unreachable by simple name from anywhere under `GridCore.Platform`, which cost fifteen minutes to discover.
- **Each bill line is rounded and the total is the sum of the printed lines.** Rounding once at the end gives a document whose lines do not add up to its own total, which is the first thing a customer checks. `Bill.Calculate` **asserts** the total equals the sum of its lines and throws otherwise — invariant 3's guard one step upstream of Finance — and refuses rather than correcting.
- **⚠️ A tariff's identity is now its code AND its effective date.** WP-0.8 keyed `ReferenceId` on the code alone, which made repricing structurally impossible. `RatePlan.KeyFor(code, effectiveFrom)` is the natural key; the indexes are `ux_rate_plans_code_effective` and `ux_rate_plans_default_effective` (filtered on `is_default`, so every *version* of the default carries the flag). `WP23_RatePlanVersions` **deletes and reinserts every reference row** — a code change is an id change, WP-1.4's precedent — which is safe only because nothing referenced `billing.rate_plans` yet. `DefaultRatePlans.AuthoredAt` is unchanged and must stay so.
- **Three rate plan rows ship now**: RES-STD from 2025-01-01 (12.50 + 0.1145/0.1385/0.1620), COM-STD from 2025-01-01 (45.00 + 0.1290/0.1105 declining), and **RES-STD repriced from 2026-07-01** (13.75 + 0.1225/0.1480/0.1735). Both originals moved back from WP-0.8's 2026-01-01, which fell *after* five of the twelve demo reading cycles — those months priced to nothing at all. Caught by the seeder test, not by reasoning.
- **`RatePlanSelector.InForceOn` is the whole of effective dating and it is pure.** A version has a start date and no end date — it runs until the next version of the same code starts, which is one fact rather than two that can disagree. **The date that chooses the tariff is the end of the billed period, never today**, or a late run silently reprices everything it touches.
- **`RateEngine` is pure and is where the money is decided.** Tier bounds are **cumulative from zero**, not per-tier allowances: 600 kWh is 500 in tier 1 and 100 in tier 2, never 600 in each. Both sides of every shipped boundary are pinned in a table. A tier covering no units produces **no line**; the standing charge is always there, which is the difference between an empty house and one that is not connected.
- **⚠️ Two more cross-module read seams, both in `Contracts/Directories/` beside WP-2.1's** (owner's call): **`IServiceAccountDirectory`** (Customers registers `ServiceAccountDirectory`) and **`IMeterReadingDirectory`** (Metering registers `MeterReadingDirectory`). Billing consumes both and has never heard of a `customers` or `metering` schema; module tests assert Billing registers neither and that each owner registers only the interface. `FindOpenAtLocationsAsync` is the batched form of WP-2.1's derivation — "the account open at the premise this meter is on" — **keyed by premise**, because the caller holds a meter's premise.
- **A read seam rather than a `MeterReadingRecorded` consumer**, deliberately: WP-2.2 raised no "cycle finished, go and bill it" event because that would be an instruction rather than a fact. A run asks the register what a cycle produced when somebody decides to bill it, which is also what lets it be re-run after the worklist is cleared.
- **⚠️ EF cannot translate a `Where` applied to a projection into a record.** Both new directories build the query as *filtered entities first, joined and projected last*; projecting first compiles fine and throws at run time. Cost two rounds of red tests. Any later directory that joins owes the same shape.
- **Billing owns the account→tariff assignment; Customers was not touched** (owner's call). `billing.account_rate_plans`, one row per account (`ux_account_rate_plans_account`), storing a **code and never a version id** — the point of effective dating is that a repricing reaches every account without reassigning anybody. **No row is an answer:** an unassigned account bills on the default, so a migrated database with no billing setup still bills correctly. Assigning the tariff an account is already on is a **no-op**, not a 409 — deliberately unlike WP-1.4's stock adjustment that agrees with the system.
- **A billing run produces DRAFTS and publishes nothing.** Issuing is the separate act that makes a bill owed and publishes **`BillIssued`** — Finance's consumer has existed since WP-0.5 and now receives real events. **A re-run skips what is already billed** rather than refusing the whole run (unlike WP-2.2's reading cycle, which 409s): re-running after clearing the exception worklist is ordinary work. `ux_bills_account_cycle` is unfiltered — an ad-hoc bill holds NULL `cycle_code` and NULLs are distinct, WP-2.2's trick.
- **A run reports what it did NOT bill and why, in words**, and those reasons are on its single audit entry. Skipped: a reading on the exception worklist (billing a high-usage reading unseen is how a transposed digit becomes a four-thousand-dollar demand), a premise with no open account (WP-2.1 seeds that case on purpose), an account never energised, and one already billed for the cycle.
- **⚠️ `RegistryNumberSeries.NextManyAsync` is new and load-bearing.** Rows added to a context but not saved are invisible to the query that issues the next number, so a batch that asked once per bill would get `BIL-000001` every time and lose all but one to the unique index. **The next module that writes a batch uses this, not a loop.**
- **The bill lifecycle is `Draft → Issued → PartiallyPaid ⇄ Overdue → Paid`, `Cancelled` from anywhere unsettled**, `Paid`/`Cancelled` terminal. No `Overdue → Issued`. `PartiallyPaid → PartiallyPaid` is **absent on purpose**, so `Bill.RecordPayment` handles a second instalment explicitly. **WP-2.4's `Adjusted` is not here yet.**
- **`Bill.RecordPayment` exists, is unit-tested, and nothing calls it** — WP-2.5 owns the consumer that reduces a balance on `PaymentApproved`. Same shape WP-1.3 left `Asset.RecordMaintenance` in for WP-3.4. An **overpayment is refused**, not absorbed: a credit is Finance's to hold (WP-2.6).
- **`Overdue` is reached by `POST /api/bills/overdue-review`, not a scheduled job.** WP-0.4's scheduler still has nothing registered on it, deliberately. `Bill.MarkOverdue` returns **false** rather than throwing when a bill is not yet due.
- **Everything a bill needs to be read is stamped on it** — account number, customer name, premise, meter number, both dial readings, the tariff's code/name/effective date, currency, and every line's own rate. Not denormalisation for speed: each belongs to a module free to change it, and re-resolving would give a customer a different bill on a second look.

- **⚠️ GridCore's first provider interface is real: `IMeterReadingProvider` (`src/Contracts/Providers/`).** The folder had existed empty since WP-0.1. A provider **reads meters and never decides what a reading means** — consumption, rollover and every exception code are computed inside Metering from what came back. The simulator lives in `src/Modules/Metering/Simulation/` and is registered **only** against the Contracts interface (a fast test asserts the concrete type is not resolvable), so production swaps it by changing one DI line. **`IPaymentProvider` (WP-2.5), `IVendorProvider` (WP-4.1) and `ICrewProvider` (WP-3.2) copy this shape.**
- **`Meter.RegisterDigits` is new and load-bearing.** Rollover cannot be computed without knowing where the dials return to zero: 99 850 → 120 is 270 units on a five-digit register and 900 270 on a six-digit one. Default 5, range 4–9, correctable through `PUT /api/meters/{id}` but never below what the meter is already recorded as having read. The migration backfills existing rows to 5 and then **drops the default**, because EF's own backfill leaves 0 and no arithmetic can run against that. `RegisterCapacity` is computed and `Ignore`d.
- **The arithmetic and the judgement are two classes.** `ConsumptionCalculator.Between(previous, current, digits)` returns units + a `RolledOver` flag and never guesses whether a wrap was genuine; `ReadingAssessment.Classify(...)` raises `HighUsage`/`ZeroUsage`/`MissingRead`. Both pure, both exhaustively unit-tested. **Everything is judged per day, never per reading period** — periods are unequal, so a two-month gap would otherwise flag a household that used exactly what it always does. `HighUsageMultiple` = 3.
- **A missing read is a row with a null `Reading`, not an absent row** — and it is **skipped** when the next reading looks for what to measure from, or an unread month reports the following period as nothing and the one after as double. `MeterReadingRecorded` is published for it too, with null reading and null consumption.
- **`ReadingBaseline.From(meter, recentAtPremise)` is pure and holds the two rules that pull opposite ways:** the previous **dial** reading comes from *this meter on its current fitting* (dials do not carry over — an exchange measures from its own installation reading), while the **usage profile** is averaged across every recent reading at the *premise*, through exchanges. The service does two queries per meter deliberately — the dial reading has to be exact, the average only needs a six-row window. Route capped at `MaxCycleSize` = 500.
- **`ux_meter_readings_meter_cycle` is unique on (meter_id, cycle_code) and unfiltered** — a manual read holds NULL and NULLs are distinct, so a premise can be re-read by hand as often as a dispute needs while a cycle run twice collides. The service pre-checks the cycle code and answers 409 naming it. Same trick as `ux_meters_service_location`; a fast test asserts the filter is absent.
- **Determinism is keyed on the meter NUMBER, not the id.** Each meter draws its own stream from (seed, cycleCode, meterNumber) via `DeterministicRandom` (SplitMix64, hand-rolled because `Random(seed)` is not documented to be stable across runtime versions). Keying on the Guid gave every freshly seeded database a different demo world — caught by the seeder test. **Copy this shape for the payment, vendor and crew simulators.**
- **A cycle leaves ONE audit entry** (action `meter_reading.cycle`, entity `metering.reading_cycle`, the seed on it so a run can be reproduced) **and one `MeterReadingRecorded` event per reading.** Invariant 1 is per write endpoint; the events are per fact.

- **A quantity is a running total and the ledger is what explains it.** `StockLevel.QuantityOnHand` is a sum; every change is a `StockMovement` with a **signed** `QuantityChange` and a stamped `QuantityOnHandAfter`, written by the aggregate so nothing can move stock without saying why. A write **loads the levels and not the ledger** — a delivery of ten connectors must not read five years of movements to append to the end — so `StockItem.Movements` is empty on a write path *by design*; the detail read includes it. If a later WP needs the ledger during a write, load it deliberately rather than adding an `Include` to `LoadAsync`.
- **The low-stock rule is written twice and held together by a test.** `StockLevel.IsBelow` (in memory) and `StockLevel.BelowMinimum` (an `Expression`, for the `?belowMinimum=` filter, because EF cannot translate the method). `StockLevelTests` runs a case table through both, compiling the expression — edit one without the other and the fast loop fails. The same pattern as WP-1.2's index-filter tests; use it for the next rule that has to exist in both forms.
- **Stock adjustments are the module's sensitive action (invariant 5).** The caller states the *counted* quantity and the difference is derived; a count that already matches is a **409** (deliberately unlike WP-1.3's re-assessment at the same condition, which *is* recorded); the reason is required from both the validator and `StockMovement.RequireReason`. `inventory.adjust` is held by Warehouse and Administrator only — a Technician or Manager holding read, or a storeman holding write, gets 403 before the handler runs.
- **Two asymmetries worth keeping straight:** a **discontinued item** refuses receipts but allows issues (the remaining stock still has to leave the shelf), and a **closed warehouse** refuses receipts and reorder levels but allows issues and adjustments (or its stock is stranded). Neither is deletable — there is no DELETE in this registry either.
- **Every movement method runs its guards before the first mutation**, and that cost a test failure to notice: an unexplained adjustment was refused *after* the shelf had moved. The transaction rolls back, so the database was safe — but the aggregate the caller still holds would report a count that never happened. Any later aggregate with a mutating method owes the same ordering.
- **A minimal-API handler taking a service without `[FromServices]` fails at route-build time**, not at request time — "Body was inferred but the method does not allow inferred body parameters". `StockItemEndpointsTests`/`AssetEndpointsTests` build the whole route table, so the fast loop catches it; the host would otherwise refuse to start. Every handler in this repo annotates its services.

- The Aspire **CLI and SDK/packages are both 13.5.2** — `aspire update` at the Phase 0 gate reported "Project is up to date" and changed nothing. Note the command needs `--yes` alongside `--non-interactive`, or it just prints help and exits 0. Still run it at each gate.
- `Web.Host` requires AppHost-supplied connection strings (`gridcore`, `redis`, `rabbitmq`) to start, **and** `Authentication:Authority` + `Authentication:Audience` (it throws a named `InvalidOperationException` without them). The gate fixture supplies all five; the stubbed authority never has to resolve, because a caller with no token is refused before any metadata fetch.
- `/health` is mapped by `MapDefaultEndpoints()` in **Development only** (Aspire's default, for the security reasons in aka.ms/aspire/healthchecks). If the demo deploy needs it exposed, that is WP-5.4.
- The Keycloak realm lives at `src/AppHost/keycloak/realms/gridcore-realm.json` and is imported **in Development only** (it carries eight test users, password `Dev!Passw0rd`). Keycloak imports a realm once into its data volume: after editing that file, `docker volume rm` the keycloak volume or the change is ignored. **WP-5.1 owes a production realm** — same roles and clients, real users, no default passwords — since nothing is imported outside Development.
- The host is now **secure by default**: a fallback policy requires an authenticated caller, so every new module endpoint needs `.RequirePermission(...)` (or a deliberate `.AllowAnonymous()`). Add new permissions to `Platform/Security/Permissions.cs` and grant them in `RolePermissionMap` — nowhere else.
- Aspire resolves Keycloak's `http` endpoint to an **https** proxy URL (`https://localhost:<port>/realms/gridcore`), so that is the issuer the SPA uses (WP-0.6 wires it through `VITE_OIDC_AUTHORITY`); using a different host for login than for token validation would break issuer matching.
- Audit and services take **`ICurrentUser`** (`Platform/Security/ICurrentUser.cs`) — never `IHttpContextAccessor` or raw claims. Outside a request it resolves to `SystemUser` (`system`, ungated); an anonymous caller inside a request holds nothing. WP-0.5 consumers get `system` attribution for free.
- MinIO is up as a container with `MinIO__Endpoint/AccessKey/SecretKey` handed to the host, but **no client is wired** — whichever WP first stores a document owns that.

- New unit-test projects must be added to `tests/UnitTests.slnf` **and** `GridCore.slnx` or the fast loop silently skips them.
- **EF Core is now in the repo** (10.0.11 + Npgsql 10.0.3, central-pinned). A module adding persistence copies `Platform/Data/PlatformDbContext.cs`: own schema via `HasDefaultSchema`, explicit snake_case names in `IEntityTypeConfiguration`, `MigrationsHistoryTable("__ef_migrations_history", <schema>)`, and an `IDesignTimeDbContextFactory` because the module is a class library.
- The fast tier runs the real EF model on **SQLite in-memory** (`tests/Platform.UnitTests/Data/PlatformTestDatabase.cs`) — copy it for module DbContexts. Two SQLite gotchas it works around: no `jsonb` (the context relaxes the column type off Npgsql) and **no `ORDER BY` on `DateTimeOffset`** (order by the Guid v7 key instead, which sorts chronologically on both providers).
- New entities use `Guid.CreateVersion7(now)` so the PK index orders chronologically. Entries created in the *same* clock instant have no defined order — a test with a frozen `FakeClock` must advance it between writes.
- **Cross-context atomicity is settled (WP-0.5).** A module registers its context with `services.AddGridCoreDbContext<TContext>((builder, connection) => builder.UseNpgsql(connection, ...))` — never plain `AddDbContext`, which would give it its own connection and its own transaction. A write then wraps itself in `IUnitOfWork.ExecuteAsync(async ct => { ... })` and **never calls `SaveChanges` itself**: the unit of work attaches every registered context to one transaction, saves them all and commits. That is what makes a module write + its audit entry + its outbox row atomic.
- `Platform` exposes internals to `GridCore.Platform.UnitTests` (`InternalsVisibleTo`) for `ScheduledJobRunner.RunOnceAsync`.
- Recurring work registers with `services.AddScheduledJob<TJob>()` (scoped, resolved per run). Nothing is registered yet — the runner starts with an empty schedule.
- **The bus is live (WP-0.5).** Publish with `IEventPublisher.PublishAsync(SomeEvent.For(...), ct)` **inside** an `IUnitOfWork.ExecuteAsync` — with MassTransit's bus outbox, that writes a row to `platform.outbox_message` rather than reaching RabbitMQ, and the delivery service ships it after the commit. Publishing without then committing publishes nothing, by design.
- Consume by deriving `IdempotentConsumer<TEvent>` and registering it from the module's `AddServices` with `services.AddEventConsumer<TConsumer>()`. `ConsumerName` is the dedupe key in `platform.processed_messages` — **stable forever**; renaming one replays every event it ever handled.
- `AddGridCoreMessaging` is called **after** `AddModules` in `Web.Host/Program.cs` because it reads the consumers back off the service collection. A module registering a consumer after that line would be silently ignored.
- **MassTransit is pinned to 8.5.10 — the last Apache-2.0 line. Never bump to 9.x** (commercial licence). Dependabot (WP-0.7) must be configured to hold it.
- New events go in `src/Contracts/Events/` as positional records implementing `IIntegrationEvent`, with a static `For(...)` that stamps `Guid.CreateVersion7(occurredAt)`. The publisher rejects an event with an empty `EventId`.
- Finance's ledger is still a **no-op**: `IJournalPostingSeam` → `LoggingJournalPostingSeam` logs the balanced entry it would post. WP-2.6 replaces the implementation; the mapping (`FinancePostings`) and the balanced-or-throw guard (`JournalPostingIntent.For`) are already real.
- The MassTransit 8.x pin is now enforced twice: `Directory.Packages.props` holds it, `.github/dependabot.yml` refuses to offer 9.x, and `DependencyPolicyTests` fails the fast loop if either is edited away.
- `tests/Contracts.UnitTests` exists now and is registered in both `GridCore.slnx` and `tests/UnitTests.slnf`.
